## Cost vs. value insights

Everything below is interpretation, not measurement. "Value" is judged only by two observable signals: whether *later sessions or commits actually build on the work* (found by grepping later transcripts for the file/system names a given day introduced), and explicit user feedback in this conversation or in the session transcripts. Cost is the supplied daily figure. Treat this as a read of the pattern, not a scorecard.

### What drove the expensive days

The three most expensive days — **2026-08-21 ($28.55)**, **2026-09-03 ($20.27)**, **2026-09-01 ($15.60)** — share a pattern that the cheap days don't: each opened with an open-ended architecture brief rather than a specific bug or bounded feature request ("we need to establish the data and entity-instantiation model," "take over an ongoing migration," "we need an easy way to orchestrate the gameplay config and data"). These are also the three days with the highest session-log line counts and token volume of the whole dataset. Open-ended "figure out the shape of this system" conversations cost several times more than pointed fixes, independent of how large the eventual diff is — 2026-08-21's session produced no single commit by itself (it was folded into the 89-file `bebd05f` bundle four days later), while 2026-09-10's four cheap sessions ($8.42 total) directly produced the 58-file `2263357` commit the same day.

The cheapest days — **2026-08-16 ($0.76)**, **2026-09-05 ($1.82)**, **2026-09-06 ($2.01)** — are all short, single-topic, narrowly-scoped sessions (7–15 minutes each): a scaffolding task, an editor metadata tweak, a camera-smoothing bug fix. Narrow scope, not necessarily low difficulty, is what kept these cheap.

Two secondary patterns:
- **Parallel sessions add, they don't discount.** 2026-09-01 ran three sessions across three working directories at once (main repo, `scripts/data`, in-progress editor). Cost scaled with total work done, not down — running things in parallel bought wall-clock time, not budget.
- **Foundation days subsidize the feature days that follow.** After the expensive Aug 21 entity-spawning push and the Sep 1 session/editor split, the Sep 9–10 Hunter-AI work (WaveRules engine, HuntingComponent design, three bug fixes) cost only $16.58 combined precisely because it had a data/session/editor substrate to build on instead of inventing one.
